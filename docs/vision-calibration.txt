Camera calibration lab

Start the existing local companion, then open http://127.0.0.1:8765/vision.
The lab starts in simulation and does not contact the device until requested.
It adds no firmware dependency and sends no keyboard or mouse input.

Workflow
1. Use Simulation to inspect the mapping without hardware, or Open image to
   inspect an existing camera photo locally.
2. Open calibration target on the monitor to be mapped. Enter full screen
   before capturing. Keep that target displayed while taking a camera frame.
3. Use Perch camera, then Freeze frame. With one monitor, capture while the
   target is visible using another browser window/display or a saved image;
   switching away from the lab pauses polling. Stop camera returns Perch Home.
4. Mark four points, clicking marker centres in order: top left, top right,
   bottom right, bottom left. Their destinations are 8%/92% of the target,
   not the physical corners of the monitor.
5. Check centre, then click the separate central cross in the camera image.
   The reported error is in target CSS pixels. Four fitted points alone do
   not validate accuracy. The centre check is one diagnostic, not a guarantee.
6. Move over the camera view to probe mapped positions. Blue is the image
   centre projection; gold is the mouse probe. Both are preview dots only.
   Export calibration downloads local JSON; images are not uploaded externally.

Limits
Manual correspondences apply to one frozen planar view. Recalibrate after
camera or target movement. New preview frames discard old calibration.
Target resize/full-screen changes also discard real-frame calibration.
The current device preview is 320x240, limiting annotation precision. Larger
saved photos offer more pixels but cannot undo optical blur.
There is no automatic marker detection, hand recognition, lens-distortion
correction, calibrated optical axis, or OS cursor control in this prototype.
A fingertip in front of the monitor is at a different depth. Screen-plane
calibration cannot infer the direction of a 3D pointing finger from its tip.
The lab introduces no tracking packages or downloaded models.

Next experiments
For handheld aiming, detect screen markers continuously and project the
camera centre, suspending output whenever markers are lost. This does not
require finger recognition, but needs adequate marker visibility and tests.
For finger-controlled pointing, stabilize the camera and track a hand in a
defined workspace; use explicit clutch/click gestures. PC-assisted landmark
processing is a separate optional experiment, not implemented by this lab.
